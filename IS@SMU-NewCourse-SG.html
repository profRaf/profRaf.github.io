🗺️ The Six-Stage Map
1
💡
IDEATE · Find the need

Purpose: Identify a curriculum need and shape an initial course concept.

Accountable: Course Proposer
Answer
  • What problem does it solve?
  • Who is the target student?
  • How does it support programme outcomes?
Produce
  • Course rationale
  • Draft title and learning outcomes
  • High-level assessment approach
2
🤝
CONSULT · Test the idea

Purpose: Validate the idea before investing effort in formal development.

Accountable: Course Proposer
Consult
  • Programme Leadership
  • Subject Matter Experts
  • Faculty teaching related courses
Check
  • Overlap with existing courses
  • Student demand
  • Available resources

Output: Stakeholder feedback and a refined course concept.

3
🧩
ALIGN · Fit the curriculum

Purpose: Determine how the course fits within the IS curriculum.

Accountable: Programme Leadership
Decide
  • Core, track course, or elective?
  • Prerequisites and exclusions?
  • Which programme outcomes?
Produce
  • Curriculum positioning
  • Competency mapping
  • Requisites and exclusions
  • Assessment framework

Design centre: This is typically where most curriculum design work occurs.

4
✅
APPROVE · Secure sign-off

Purpose: Obtain academic and governance approval.

Accountable: School Academic Leadership
Engage
  • School Academic Leadership
  • Registrar's Office
  • Relevant curriculum committees
Check
  • Academic soundness
  • Curriculum policy alignment
  • Governance support

Output: Approved course proposal and governance sign-off.

5
📝
CREATE · Record the course

Purpose: Create the official course record.

Accountable: Course Owner
Support
  • Curriculum Administration
  • CCMS / Coursedog
Capture
  • Title and description
  • Learning outcomes
  • Assessment and competencies
  • Credits, requisites, delivery information

Output: Official course record in CCMS.

6
🚀
OFFER · Enable enrolment

Purpose: Prepare the course for student enrolment.

Accountable: Academic Operations
Coordinate
  • Undergraduate Administration
  • Timetabling Team
  • Programme Leadership
  • Teaching Faculty
Activate
  • Scheduling
  • Faculty assignment
  • Catalogue publication
  • Registration setup

Output: Published course with student enrolment enabled.

Worked example: A faculty member notices that students need stronger AI product management skills. They ideate the course, consult colleagues about demand and overlap, align it as an elective with suitable outcomes and requisites, obtain approval, create the official record, and then the course is offered through scheduling and registration setup.
🩴 Chancleta Alerts · Avoid These Process Errors

🚫 Do not start with the form

Formal data entry is not the same as curriculum design.

Form first → hope the fit emerges Need → consultation → curricular alignment → form

⚠️ Do not skip the fit check

A compelling topic may still duplicate an existing course or lack a clear curriculum position.

“Students will like it” Show demand, distinctiveness, outcomes, and resources

🚫 Do not confuse roles

The proposer develops the idea. Programme Leadership establishes curricular fit. Operations prepares delivery.

One person owns every decision Accountability moves as the workflow advances

⚠️ Approval is not enrolment

Academic approval creates permission. Scheduling, publication, and registration make the course available to students.

Approved = automatically enrolable Approve → create record → offer course

🧠 Retrieval Practice

A · New idea A faculty member identifies a curriculum gap.
B · Curriculum fit The team decides whether it is core, track, or elective.
C · Official record The course data is entered into CCMS / Coursedog.
D · Student access The course is scheduled and registration is enabled.
Answers: A = Ideate · B = Align · C = Create · D = Offer
