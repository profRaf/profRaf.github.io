One question it answers How do I design a persona that acts like a real SMU student?
"A great persona is defined by their anxieties and competing priorities, not just their goals."
🧱 What To Do: The 4 Pillars of Reality
1
Contextualise the Roots
Ground them in exact academic pathways instead of just calling them "smart."

Example: Jia Hui's H2 Math/Econ vs. John's Poly IT Diploma dictates their technical confidence and whether repeating basic coding is a dealbreaker.
2
Surface the Friction
Identify their vulnerabilities and conflicting motivations. Do not just list career goals.

Example: The ROI opportunity cost (a Poly grad giving up a $40k job offer) or Status Anxiety (peers viewing CS as the only "real" tech degree).
3
Factor in the NS Gap
For male students, the 2-year National Service gap is critical context, not a footnote.

Example: Marcus feels academically rusty, relies on strict weekend book-outs, and scrolls IG comparison vlogs at 1:00 AM on his bunk bed.
4
Pinpoint Niche Channels
Move past generic terms like "Social Media" or "The Internet." Map granular, actionable touchpoints.

Example: Lurking on r/SGExams, DM'ing SCIS seniors on LinkedIn, or reading NUSWhispers threads.
🩴 Chancleta Alerts (What to Avoid)

1. The "Flat" Stereotype

Labeling a student "unaware" without explaining the root cause of the ignorance.

❌ "David doesn't know tech." ✅ "His JC lumps all IT together and nobody in his family works in tech."

2. Corporate Jargon

Writing user needs or quotes using abstract university prospectus copy.

❌ "Needs a synergistic degree option." ✅ "Why waste four years and $40k to relearn basic coding?"

3. Super-Student Bias

Creating a flawless coder/speaker with zero emotional hang-ups or fears.

❌ "Exceptional coder and public speaker." ✅ "Mathematically brilliant, but too anxious to speak up in seminars."

4. The Vacuum Trap

Forgetting that parents act as major financial gatekeepers and influencers.

❌ Student makes choice 100% alone. ✅ Parents push for "safe" finance degrees due to fears of AI automation.
