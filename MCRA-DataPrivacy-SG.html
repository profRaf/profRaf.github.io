PDPA vs GDPR 🧭

SMU Information Systems · Personal Data Protection · Singapore and EU comparison
One question it answers What changes when personal data crosses Singapore and the EU?
Running example: An SMU student registers for an event. SMU collects the student’s name and email, stores the data in Singapore, and uses an EU-based email platform to send event updates.

Who and where?
Singapore
Applies mainly to organisations handling personal data in Singapore. The PDPA has Singapore-specific obligations and can apply to organisations outside Singapore in some circumstances.
EU
Applies to controllers and processors established in the EU, and can apply outside the EU when offering goods or services to people in the EU or monitoring their behaviour there.
Legal basis
Consent is central, but the PDPA also recognises deemed consent and exceptions such as legitimate interests, business improvement, and other statutory exceptions.
Processing needs one of six legal bases, including consent, contract, legal obligation, vital interests, public task, or legitimate interests. Consent must be specific, informed, and freely given.
Individual rights
Core rights include access to personal data and information about its use, plus correction. Singapore’s data portability obligation is not treated as a general operational equivalent of the GDPR right in this guide.
Broader rights include access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability, objection, and safeguards concerning solely automated decisions, subject to conditions and exceptions.
Data breach
Notify the PDPC and affected individuals when a breach is notifiable. Notification to the PDPC must be made as soon as practicable and no later than three calendar days after determining that the breach is notifiable.
Notify the competent supervisory authority without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours of becoming aware of a risk-relevant breach. Notify individuals without undue delay when the risk is high.
Privacy officer
Every organisation must designate at least one Data Protection Officer and make the DPO’s business contact information available.
A Data Protection Officer is mandatory only in specified situations, such as public-authority processing, large-scale regular monitoring, or large-scale sensitive-data processing.
Cross-border transfer
The organisation must ensure the transferred data receives a standard of protection comparable to the PDPA. Contractual and other safeguards may be used.
Transfers outside the EEA require an adequacy decision, appropriate safeguards such as Standard Contractual Clauses, or a limited derogation.
Maximum penalty
For qualifying organisations, financial penalties can reach the higher of S$1 million or 10% of annual turnover in Singapore.
Administrative fines can reach the higher of €20 million or 4% of worldwide annual turnover, depending on the infringement.

🧪 Apply it to the SMU event example

Singapore collection → PDPA duties apply → EU email platform → assess transfer safeguards → EU residents targeted or monitored → GDPR may also apply.
The same activity can trigger both regimes. Do not assume that compliance with one automatically proves compliance with the other.
Exam-Ready Sentence The PDPA and GDPR both regulate responsible handling of personal data, but the GDPR generally provides a broader rights-and-bases framework with stronger extraterritorial reach, while the PDPA uses Singapore-specific consent, accountability, transfer, and breach-notification obligations.
🩴 Chancleta Alerts: fatal errors

1. “Consent is everything”

Both laws recognise alternatives and exceptions. The exact legal basis must be identified for the specific processing activity.

Consent checkbox = automatic compliance State purpose, basis, notice, and safeguards

2. “PDPA = Singapore GDPR”

The regimes overlap, but their rights, exceptions, regulator expectations, transfer rules, and penalty structures differ.

Use the same checklist unchanged Map the activity against both laws

3. “72 hours applies everywhere”

GDPR uses a 72-hour supervisory-authority rule. Singapore’s PDPA uses a different notification trigger and three-calendar-day outer deadline after the determination.

One global breach clock Identify jurisdiction, trigger, and recipient
