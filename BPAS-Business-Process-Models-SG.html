Which model do I use? 🧭

Business Process Modelling · Select the model by its purpose, not one tempting keyword
One question it answers What is this question
really trying to explain?
Fast rule: Read the whole question. Then ask what information the answer must reveal: structure, place, things involved, work happening, or logic controlling work. The same noun, such as “manager”, “system”, or “approval”, can appear in more than one model.
1 · Match the purpose to the model
If the question asks...
Primary model
Diagnostic cue
“Who reports to whom?”
“Which unit owns this team?”
Organisation · Why
Structure and hierarchy. Reporting line, department, business unit, role placement.
“Where does the business or solution operate?”
“Which country hosts the service?”
Location · Where
Geography and deployment context. Country, branch, site, region, time zone, regulation.
“Who or what is involved?”
“Which people, systems, or suppliers are needed?”
Resource · Who
Actors and assets. Humans, technologies, internal/external parties, capabilities.
“Who performs what, in what order, and how long?”
Workflow · How, When
Activities and movement. Sequence, hand-offs, swimlanes, gateways, timing.
“What condition, formula, policy, or criterion applies?”
Business Rules · Governance
Logic governing an activity. IF/THEN, eligibility, threshold, validation, calculation.
2 · Use the Subway order case to resolve ambiguity
👥 “The sandwich artist handles orders.”
Resource if the point is that a sandwich artist is a required human participant.
Ask: “Who is involved?” → Resource
➡️ “The sandwich artist checks the order, then prepares it.”
Process Workflow if the point is activities, sequence, and responsibility.
Ask: “Who does what next?” → Workflow
🏢 “The store manager supervises sandwich artists.”
Organisation if the point is authority or reporting relationship, not simply that both are people.
Ask: “Who reports to whom?” → Organisation
✅ “A manager approval is required above $100.”
Business Rules if the point is the threshold that determines whether approval is needed.
Ask: “Under what condition?” → Business Rules
🩴 Chancleta Alerts · Do not classify by keyword alone

“Person” does not always mean Resource

A person can be an actor, a step owner, or a manager in a hierarchy.

“Manager” = Resource Purpose: actor / sequence / reporting?

“Approval” does not always mean Workflow

Approval may be an activity. The threshold triggering it is a rule.

“Approval” = Workflow Action = Workflow; condition = Rule

Do not force one fact into one model

Complementary models can describe the same case from different angles.

Pick the “best keyword” Pick the question's intended view
Exam-Ready Sentence: “I select the [model] because the question seeks to describe [structure / location / participants / sequence of work / governing logic], rather than merely mentioning a related keyword.”

🧠 Retrieval Practice

Name the primary model first. Then say why in five words or fewer.
1Which teams report to the Head of Operations?
2Which POS system and delivery platform are involved?
3After payment, who packs the order and when is it collected?
4When must an order be escalated to a manager?
Answers: 1 Organisation: reporting structure | 2 Resource: technologies involved | 3 Process Workflow: activities, responsibility, timing | 4 Business Rules: escalation condition
